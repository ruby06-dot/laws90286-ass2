Southland, a WTO Member, prohibits the sale of all seal products (such as seal skin, seal oil and seal meat products) on its market. The prohibition does not apply to seal products that come from hunts conducted by indigenous communities for subsistence, or from small-scale hunts conducted for the sole purpose of sustainable marine resource management. The prohibition applies equally to domestic and imported seal products and does not mention origin. Seal products from all types of hunts are physically identical, are used for the same purposes, and fall under the same tariff headings. In practice, nearly all seal products produced in Southland come from small-scale marine resource management hunts and therefore qualify for the exception. By contrast, nearly all seal products from Eastland, the main foreign supplier, come from large-scale commercial hunts and do not qualify. Southland explains that the measure responds to public moral concerns about animal welfare in commercial seal hunting.
