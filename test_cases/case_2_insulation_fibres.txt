Eastmark, a WTO Member, has banned the sale and use in buildings of insulation products containing Fibre K, a mineral fibre. Scientific bodies, including the World Health Organization, classify Fibre K as a known human carcinogen, and studies show that inhaling Fibre K particles during installation or demolition causes serious lung disease. Insulation products containing Fibre M, a synthetic fibre for which there is no evidence of carcinogenic effects, remain lawful. Almost all Fibre K insulation sold in Eastmark was imported from Northland. Most Fibre M insulation is made in Eastmark. Both types of insulation are used for the same purpose in building construction and have similar strength and heat resistance. They are classified under different tariff headings. Construction companies in Eastmark told a government inquiry that they increasingly avoid Fibre K products because of liability and worker safety concerns.
