Westland, a WTO Member, has adopted the Beef Retail Regulation. Under the Regulation, fresh and chilled beef imported from other countries may only be sold in specialist retail stores that are licensed to sell imported beef and that do not sell domestic beef. Domestic beef may be sold in any supermarket, butcher or other retail outlet. Before the Regulation, most supermarkets in Westland sold both imported and domestic beef side by side. Since the Regulation took effect, about 5,000 specialist imported-beef stores have opened, compared with more than 45,000 general retail outlets that sell domestic beef. Imported and domestic beef are cuts of the same cattle species, are used in the same dishes, and are classified under the same Harmonized System heading (0201). Westland states that the Regulation is intended to prevent consumers from being misled about the origin of beef.
